AC Leak Water Damage in Wyoming: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Insulation Around the Unit

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.
