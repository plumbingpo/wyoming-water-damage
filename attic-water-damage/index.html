Attic Water Damage in Wyoming

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Wyoming, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Light from outside visible along a roof valley or around a chimney or vent pipe
  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Air Conditioner Drips

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Ceiling Collapse

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Water Near Electrical

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.
