A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in Wyoming, Wyoming Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

After extraction and sanitizing, we set up commercial drying equipment, including LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, to dry the structure of your property thoroughly. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly

The Benefits of Professional Flood Cleanup

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Protecting your family from E. coli, hepatitis and similar waterborne illness takes proper containment, correct use of protective equipment (PPE) and proven sanitizing methods, all things professional teams train for.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.
