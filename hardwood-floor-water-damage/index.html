Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Wyoming Homeowners Should Know

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Warning Signs

  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • A springy or soft spot in the floor that was not there earlier
  • Baseboards or door trim swelling where the floor meets the wall
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Remove Surface Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Set Drying Mats

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under Boards

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

Heavy airflow or heat dries the face of a board well ahead of its core. Lasting gaps and split or cracked boards are the usual outcome.

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

If cupped boards get sanded level before they dry, the high edges are ground away. After the moisture leaves, each board is left with a raised center, which is crowning.

Wet Subfloor

Dry feeling boards can sit on a subfloor that remains damp for a long time. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.
