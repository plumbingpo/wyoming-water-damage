Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Wyoming

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Wyoming, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

Our Process

How We Handle Appliance Leak Water Damage

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

After making sure the supply is shut and moving the disconnected appliance, our crew follows the water with meters and a thermal camera into nearby walls, under flooring and beneath cabinets.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

Any surface that dishwasher or washer discharge reached is treated with an antimicrobial. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.

What Happens if You Wait

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.
