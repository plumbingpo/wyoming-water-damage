Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Wyoming

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

What Happens After You Call

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.
  • How deep the water got and how much of the basement it covered.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.
