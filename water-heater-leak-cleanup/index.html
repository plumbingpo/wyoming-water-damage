Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Wyoming

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Wyoming crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
