Crawl Space Water Removal in Wyoming

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.
