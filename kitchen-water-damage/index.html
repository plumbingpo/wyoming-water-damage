Kitchen Water Damage Help in Wyoming

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Wyoming, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.
