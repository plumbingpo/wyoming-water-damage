Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Wyoming Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Wyoming

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

What to Expect From Our Structural Drying

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
