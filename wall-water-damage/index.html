Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Wyoming Homeowners Should Know

Drywall acts like a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
